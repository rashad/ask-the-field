# SOP-OPS-009 Shift Handover and Daily Shift Report

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Procedure ID | SOP-OPS-009 |
| Title | Shift Handover and Daily Shift Report |
| Revision date | 2025-06-30 |
| Applies to | All three fields: Dune Field, Wadi Field and Salt Flat Field |
| Owner | Production Supervisor |
| Users | Lead Operators, Night Lead Operators, Field Engineers, Production Engineers |

## Purpose and scope

This procedure sets out how operating shifts hand over responsibility for the wells, flowlines and the central processing facility CPF-1, and how the daily shift report is written and issued. Its aim is that the incoming crew knows the true state of every well and every open job before it takes control, and that production figures are recorded in one consistent way.

The procedure applies to all three fields: Dune Field, Wadi Field and Salt Flat Field. It covers all producing methods in use on the asset (ESP, gas lift and natural flow) and all well statuses (producing, shut-in, workover and suspended). It applies to day and night shifts, and to weekends and holidays without exception.

Responsibilities:

- **Outgoing Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator:** prepares the shift report, briefs the incoming crew and stays on site until handover is signed.
- **Incoming Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator:** reviews the report, verifies the key points in the field or on the control system, and accepts the shift only when satisfied.
- **Production Supervisor:** resolves disagreements at handover, reviews reports daily and audits compliance.
- **Production Engineer and Field Engineer:** provide technical input on restricted wells and open incidents when asked.
- **HSE Advisor:** is informed of any safety-relevant item recorded in the report.

## Handover meeting

1. Handover takes place face to face in the control room, at the scheduled shift change. A phone handover is allowed only when the outgoing person cannot be present for a safety reason, and the Production Supervisor must be told.
2. Allow at least 20 minutes. Do not start the meeting while an alarm is being worked, unless the alarm is the first item discussed.
3. The outgoing Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator walks through the written shift report section by section, using the control system trends on screen for the main points.
4. The incoming Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator confirms understanding by repeating back all items that affect safety, any well that is restricted or offline, and all active permits to work.
5. Both people walk the CPF-1 status board together and check that it matches the report: separator levels, export status, flare, power generation and any equipment in bypass or override.
6. Any active permit to work, isolation or lock-out must be named and its location confirmed. The incoming person must see the permit paperwork before accepting the shift.
7. Items that cannot be verified during the meeting are marked "to verify" in the report and checked within the first hour of the shift.
8. Both people sign the report. The incoming Lead Operator takes control only after signing. Signing means the incoming person has understood the state of the plant, not merely received the paper.
9. Field operators and the maintenance crew on shift receive a short briefing from the incoming Lead Operator within 30 minutes of handover, covering tasks, hazards and permits for the shift.

If the two people disagree about the state of a well or item of equipment, the item is checked in the field together before signature. If the disagreement remains, the Production Supervisor is called.

## Contents of the shift report

The shift report is written by the outgoing Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator before the handover meeting, using the standard template. It must be terse, factual and free of opinion that cannot be supported by a reading. The following sections are mandatory:

1. **Header:** date in the format YYYY-MM-DD, shift (day or night), names by role and initial, and weather or site conditions that affected work.
2. **Production summary:** oil, gas and water totals for each field and for CPF-1 for the shift, with a short remark on any trend that differs from the previous shift.
3. **Wells offline or restricted:** see the next section.
4. **Events:** trips, alarms that required action, process upsets, flaring above normal, power dips, leaks, spills and near misses, each with time, action taken and current state.
5. **Routine work:** well checks, sampling, chemical injection rounds, valve exercising, slickline or wireline activity, maintenance and inspections completed, plus any that were planned but not done and the reason.
6. **Permits and isolations:** all permits to work open at shift end, with location and holder.
7. **Chemicals and consumables:** tank levels for demulsifier, corrosion inhibitor, scale inhibitor and diesel, and any delivery expected.
8. **Handover notes:** actions for the incoming shift, instructions received from supervisors or engineers, and items marked "to verify".

Use plain units: oil in bbl/d (or bbl for a shift or a day), gas in Mscf/d, water cut in percent, pressure in psi, sand in ppm and choke in 64ths of an inch. Do not mix units within the report. Write "not measured" where a reading was not taken; never leave a field blank and never copy forward a reading from an earlier shift without saying so.

## Wells offline and open incidents

Every report must list each well that is not producing normally at shift end. For each entry record:

- Well name and field.
- Status: shut-in, workover, suspended, or producing under restriction.
- Reason, and the time it went offline or was restricted.
- Choke, ESP frequency or gas lift rate currently set, if the well is restricted.
- Last known rate before the change and an estimate of deferred oil for the shift, marked as an estimate.
- Next action, who owns it and when it is due.

Open incidents are listed separately, with the incident note reference, current status and the next step. An incident stays on the report until the production engineer or HSE Advisor has marked it closed in the incident note. The operating shift never closes an incident by its own decision.

Rules for wells:

1. A well is not restarted after a trip, a shut-in for safety or a downhole alarm without the approval named in the relevant operating procedure. The report records who approved the restart and when.
2. If a restricted well is returned to full operation, the report states the new settings and the time of the change.
3. Wells under workover are reported with the current stage of the job as given by the Workover Supervisor. Wells that are suspended are listed each day so that their status is not forgotten.
4. For Salt Flat Field wells, record sand readings and any change in the sand-handling setup. For Wadi Field wells, record any change in water cut seen on the daily sample or the test separator, since water cut there is expected to keep rising.

## Production figures and sources

Production figures in the shift report must come from approved sources, in the following order of preference:

1. **Fiscal or allocated metering at CPF-1** for field and facility totals, taken at the agreed cut-off time of the shift.
2. **Well test data** from the test separator for individual well rates, using the latest valid test. State the test date when quoting a well rate.
3. **Control system readings** for instantaneous values such as pressures, temperatures, choke positions, ESP frequency, motor current and gas lift injection rate.
4. **Manual readings and samples** for water cut (centrifuge or laboratory), sand and tank dips, with the time and method noted.

Rules:

- Never quote a rate from memory or from the previous report without marking it "carried forward".
- Where a meter is out of service or reading suspect, say so in the report, use the next source in the list and mark the figure "estimated".
- Round oil to the nearest bbl, gas to the nearest Mscf and water cut to the nearest whole percent unless a test report gives more precision.
- Differences between the sum of well rates and the CPF-1 total are expected. If the difference exceeds 5 percent for the shift, record it and tell the Production Engineer.
- Corrections to figures already issued are made by a dated addendum, not by overwriting the original report.

## Escalation rules

The Lead Operator or Night Lead Operator escalates as follows, and records the call time and the person informed in the report.

| Situation | Inform | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Injury, fire, gas release, spill or any event with safety or environmental impact | HSE Advisor and Production Supervisor | Immediately, then confirm in writing |
| Loss of CPF-1 export, a full facility trip or a prolonged flare event | Production Supervisor | Immediately |
| Unplanned shut-in of any well | Production Engineer | Within 1 hour |
| Downhole alarm or abnormal behavior of an ESP well, such as repeated trips or unstable current | Field Engineer and Production Engineer | Within 1 hour |
| Sand above the agreed limit at Salt Flat Field | Production Engineer | Within 2 hours |
| Unexpected jump in water cut at a Wadi Field well | Production Engineer | Same shift |
| Permit or isolation problem, or work outside the permit scope | Production Supervisor and HSE Advisor | Immediately, stop the work |
| Handover disagreement not resolved in the field | Production Supervisor | Before signing |
| Late or missing shift report from the previous shift | Production Supervisor | Start of shift |

If the person to be informed cannot be reached within 15 minutes, call the next person in the on-call list and note the attempt. A well or item of plant is never left in an uncertain state only because the right person did not answer.

The Production Supervisor reviews the shift reports each morning, checks that wells offline and open incidents are carried forward correctly, and reports any recurring gaps at the weekly operations meeting. This procedure is reviewed at least once a year, or sooner after an incident in which handover was a contributing factor.
