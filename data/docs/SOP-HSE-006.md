# SOP-HSE-006 Flowline Leak Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Procedure ID | SOP-HSE-006 |
| Title | Flowline Leak Response |
| Revision date | 2025-08-25 |
| Applies to | All three fields: Field A, Field B and Bayda |
| Owner | Production Supervisor |
| Reviewers | HSE Advisor, Integrity Engineer, Workover Supervisor |

## Purpose and scope

1. This procedure sets out how the asset responds to a suspected or confirmed leak on any production flowline between a wellhead and the inlet manifold of CPF-1, including the sections inside the field manifold areas.
2. It applies to all three fields: Field A, Field B and Bayda. It covers oil, gas and produced water flowlines, and gas lift supply lines when a leak affects the flowline corridor.
3. The aims, in order of priority, are: protect people, stop the release, contain the fluid, restore a safe and verified line, and record the event so that the cause can be addressed.
4. Bayda lines carry high GOR fluid with sand. Operators must treat any leak there as a potential gas release and as a possible erosion failure until proven otherwise.
5. This procedure does not cover leaks inside CPF-1 process areas, which follow the facility emergency response plan. It does not replace the permit to work system, the isolation and lockout procedure or the emergency response plan. Where these conflict with this SOP, the more restrictive requirement applies.

Responsibilities:

- Lead Operator: first response, isolation, initial reporting, and handover of the status.
- Production Supervisor: overall control of the response, decisions on shut-in and restart.
- HSE Advisor: spill assessment, notifications, and the incident record.
- Integrity Engineer: repair method approval, clamp and pressure test acceptance.
- Maintenance Planner: work order, materials and crew scheduling.

## Leak detection sources

A leak may be identified from any of the following sources. Any person who observes one must report it to the Lead Operator immediately by radio.

1. **Visual observation.** Staining of sand, wet ground, a visible spray, bubbling in standing water, or dead vegetation-free patches along the corridor found during operator rounds.
2. **Odour or gas alarm.** Hydrocarbon smell, or a portable or fixed gas detector alarm near a wellhead, manifold or flowline crossing. Treat a portable detector reading above 10 percent of the lower explosive limit as a confirmed release.
3. **Pressure trend.** A flowing wellhead pressure drop of more than 10 percent over 15 minutes with no choke change, or a sustained divergence between the wellhead pressure and the CPF-1 inlet pressure for the same line.
4. **Rate or balance anomaly.** A sudden fall in the metered rate at CPF-1 that is not explained by a well trip, or a mismatch between the well test rate and the allocated rate that appears abruptly.
5. **Routine inspection.** Findings from corrosion and erosion surveys, thickness readings or walkdowns by the Integrity Engineer or Field Engineer, including weeping at flanges, clamps or small-bore connections.
6. **Third party report.** A report from a vehicle crew, contractor or security patrol working in the corridor.

On any report, the Lead Operator sends an operator to verify from a safe upwind position with a portable gas detector. Do not approach a visible spray or an area with an alarming detector without breathing apparatus and a second person standing by. If the report cannot be verified quickly and the pressure evidence is strong, proceed to isolation without waiting for visual confirmation.

## Immediate isolation

1. The Lead Operator declares a flowline leak on the radio channel and informs the Production Supervisor and the CPF-1 control room.
2. Stop all hot work, vehicle movement and ignition sources within 50 m of the leak, and within 100 m downwind if gas is released. Establish an exclusion zone with barrier tape or vehicles parked outside it.
3. Shut in the well or wells feeding the affected line from the wellhead or the control room. For ESP wells, stop the pump through the drive and close the wing valve. For gas lift wells, also close the lift gas injection valve. For natural flow wells, close the wing and the choke manifold valve.
4. Close the line isolation valve at the field manifold or CPF-1 inlet end, then the valve at the wellhead end, to segregate the leaking section.
5. Depressure the isolated section to the closed drain or flare only on instruction from the Production Supervisor. Do not open vents to atmosphere where a gas release is possible.
6. Confirm isolation by pressure gauge readings on both sides and record the time of isolation. Apply tags and locks in line with the isolation and lockout procedure.
7. Check neighbouring lines in the same corridor for damage or exposure, and check that the shut-in has not caused overpressure on any shared header.
8. The Production Supervisor records the wells shut in and the expected deferment and passes the information to the Production Engineer.

## Spill containment and reporting

1. Once the line is isolated and the area is safe, the HSE Advisor assesses the spill, estimating the area and the volume released from the line inventory, the time to isolation and the rate before shut-in.
2. Contain the spill with sand berms, using the earth moving equipment on site, to stop spreading toward drainage paths or low ground. Use absorbent pads for small releases and a vacuum truck to recover free liquid into a tank at CPF-1.
3. Do not wash residual oil into the soil. Remove contaminated sand into marked containers and store it at the designated contaminated soil area pending treatment.
4. Take photographs with a scale reference and record GPS coordinates, wind direction and the extent of the stained area.
5. Notify within the specified times:
   - Production Supervisor and CPF-1 control room: immediately.
   - Asset management: within 1 hour for any release that leaves the pad or exceeds 1 bbl, or any gas release with an alarm.
   - The regulator: as required by the concession reporting rules, with the HSE Advisor making the call and keeping a record of the time and the person informed.
6. The HSE Advisor opens an incident record the same day, with the Production Engineer adding deferred oil and the well list. The record stays open until the repair is accepted and the site remediation is signed off.

## Temporary clamp repair

A temporary clamp may be used only where the Integrity Engineer has assessed the defect and approved it in writing.

1. Eligibility: the defect is a localized pinhole or small crack, the pipe wall around it has adequate remaining thickness (at least 60 percent of nominal at the clamp seating area, confirmed by ultrasonic readings), and the leak is not at a weld seam, a bend with active erosion or a flange face.
2. Ineligible cases, including a split, a longitudinal crack, general thinning or a leak at Bayda erosion points with high sand, go directly to permanent repair.
3. Obtain a permit to work and a gas test certificate. The line must be depressured, isolated and verified at zero pressure before the surface is prepared, unless the Integrity Engineer approves an on-line clamp with additional controls and the Production Supervisor signs it off.
4. Clean the pipe surface to bare metal at the clamp location, measure the outside diameter at three points and select a clamp rated above the line design pressure.
5. Install the clamp with a new sealing element, tightening the bolts evenly in a cross pattern to the torque in the vendor datasheet, then retighten after 30 minutes.
6. Pressure test the clamp at 1.1 times the normal operating pressure for 30 minutes with no visible leak and no pressure drop. The Integrity Engineer witnesses and signs the test record.
7. Register the clamp in the integrity database with its location, date, defect description and a limit of use. A temporary clamp must be replaced by a permanent repair within 30 days, and sooner if any weeping or pressure loss is seen.
8. Increase the inspection frequency at the clamp to once per shift until it is replaced.

## Permanent repair and pressure test

1. The Maintenance Planner raises a work order referencing the incident record, schedules the crew and orders pipe, fittings and weld consumables.
2. The Integrity Engineer defines the repair scope: cut-out and replacement of the damaged spool, or a full sleeve repair when allowed by the repair standard. The replacement pipe must match or exceed the material grade, wall thickness and pressure rating of the original line. For Bayda lines, consider an increased wall or an erosion-resistant bend where sand is the cause.
3. Isolate, depressure, drain and purge the line. Blind the isolation points where possible and obtain gas test certificates before hot work.
4. Perform welding under a qualified procedure with certified welders. Carry out non-destructive testing of all new welds, with 100 percent radiography or equivalent examination of tie-in welds.
5. Remove the temporary clamp, if any, with the damaged section and keep it for failure analysis.
6. Hydrotest the repaired section with clean water at 1.25 times the design pressure, holding for at least 4 hours. Accept the test only if there is no pressure loss beyond temperature correction and no visible leak. Where a hydrotest is not practicable for a short tie-in, the Integrity Engineer may approve a pneumatic leak test at a lower pressure with extra exclusion distances, plus full NDT.
7. Drain and dry the line after the test, disposing of test water at the approved facility.
8. The Integrity Engineer signs the test certificate and updates the integrity records, including wall thickness readings at the repair.

## Return to service

1. Before restart, the Production Supervisor confirms that the repair is accepted, the work order is closed out, permits are cancelled and locks and tags are removed.
2. The HSE Advisor confirms that the spill area is cleaned, berms removed or secured and the contaminated sand handled, or that the remaining work is scheduled.
3. Reinstate the line by opening the isolation valves slowly, equalizing pressure first. Re-pressurize in steps of about 25 percent of operating pressure, checking for leaks at each step with a portable detector and by visual inspection.
4. Restart the wells one at a time, starting with the lowest rate well on the line. ESP wells follow the standard restart sequence, and gas lift wells resume lift gas only after flow is established. Watch wellhead pressure, line pressure and CPF-1 inlet readings for stability.
5. Keep an operator at the repair location during the first hour of flow, then inspect hourly for the first shift.
6. Check sand production on Bayda wells during the ramp-up, and hold choke changes if sand rises above the normal alarm level.
7. The Lead Operator records the restart time and the final well list in the shift report and informs the Production Engineer, who finalizes the deferred oil figure.
8. The HSE Advisor closes the incident record only after the repair, test certificate and site remediation are all confirmed. A lessons learned note is circulated to the three fields within 14 days.
