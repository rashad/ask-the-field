# SOP-HSE-006 Flowline Leak Response

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| Procedure ID | SOP-HSE-006 |
| Title | Flowline Leak Response |
| Revision date | 2025-08-25 |
| Applies to | All three fields: Dune Field, Wadi Field and Salt Flat Field |
| Owner | Production Supervisor |
| Reviewers | HSE Advisor, Integrity Engineer, Production Engineer |

## Purpose and scope

1. This procedure sets out the actions required when a leak is suspected or confirmed on any production flowline between a wellhead and the inlet manifold of CPF-1, including tie-ins, spools, valves and flanged connections on the flowline route.
2. Its objectives are to protect people, stop the release, limit environmental impact, restore containment with a controlled repair and return the line to service only when integrity is demonstrated.
3. It applies to Dune Field, Wadi Field and Salt Flat Field. Fluid characteristics differ by field and must be considered in the hazard assessment:
   - Salt Flat Field flowlines carry high GOR fluid with a sand-prone profile. Expect gas release, flammable atmosphere risk and erosion as a possible leak cause.
   - Wadi Field flowlines carry rising water cut under water flood. Internal corrosion is a credible cause and produced water must be treated as a contaminant.
   - Dune Field flowlines carry mixed fluids from ESP, gas lift and natural flow wells.
4. Gas, H2S and fire response are covered by the site emergency response plan. If that plan is activated, it takes precedence over this procedure.
5. Roles:
   - Lead Operator: first responder, isolation and area control.
   - Night Lead Operator: same duties as the Lead Operator on night shift.
   - Production Supervisor: incident lead, authorises repair method and return to service.
   - HSE Advisor: containment, reporting, gas testing and permit review.
   - Integrity Engineer: repair method, pressure test design and acceptance.
   - Maintenance Planner: work orders, materials and contractor scheduling.
   - Production Engineer: deferred production estimate and restart sequence.

## Leak detection sources

A leak may be identified through any of the following. Every report must be treated as genuine until disproved.

1. **Visual observation.** Staining, wet sand, pooled liquid, vapour or a hydrocarbon odour reported by operators on rounds, drivers or contractors.
2. **Pressure trends.** A sustained drop in flowline pressure at the wellhead, or a divergence between wellhead and manifold pressure, not explained by choke changes or well behaviour. Operators should treat an unexplained drop of more than 10 percent of normal operating pressure within 15 minutes as a possible leak.
3. **Rate imbalance.** A shortfall between summed well test rates and CPF-1 inlet measurement that grows over consecutive shifts.
4. **Gas detection.** Portable or fixed detector alarms along the flowline corridor, manifold areas or wellsite fences.
5. **Integrity inspection findings.** Wall thickness readings below the minimum allowable value, active corrosion under insulation or erosion hot spots on bends, particularly in sand-prone service.
6. **Third-party reports.** Reports from any person on the concession, which must be logged with the time and location given.

Whoever receives a report must inform the Lead Operator immediately by radio. The Lead Operator records the time, location, source and observed conditions in the shift log.

## Immediate isolation

1. **Stop and assess from a safe position.** Do not approach a suspected leak until wind direction is established and the approach path is upwind. Carry a personal gas monitor and have a second person in radio contact.
2. **Gas check.** If the monitor reads 10 percent LEL or more, or any H2S reading is above the alarm set point, withdraw, raise the alarm and follow the emergency response plan.
3. **Notify.** The Lead Operator informs the Production Supervisor and the HSE Advisor. The CPF-1 control room operator is told which well or wells feed the affected line.
4. **Shut in the source.**
   1. For ESP wells, stop the pump from the controller and close the wing valve.
   2. For gas lift wells, close the gas lift injection valve first, then the wing valve.
   3. For natural flow wells, close the wing valve and, if required, the master valve.
5. **Isolate the line.** Close the manifold inlet valve for the affected line at CPF-1. Where the line has a sectionalising valve, close it to limit the inventory that can drain to the leak point.
6. **Depressure.** Depressurise the isolated section to the flare or closed drain system in a controlled manner. Do not vent to atmosphere. Record the pressure at the start and end of the depressuring.
7. **Lock and tag.** Apply lock and tag to all isolation points. The Production Supervisor confirms the isolation list and records it in the incident log.
8. **Control the area.** Set up a barrier at a minimum radius of 30 m, or larger if gas readings require it. Remove ignition sources and stop vehicle movement in the area.
9. **Note shut-in time.** The Lead Operator records the time of the shut-in so that deferred production can be calculated.

## Spill containment and reporting

1. **Contain.** Build sand berms or use portable bunds to stop liquid spreading, giving priority to drainage paths and low points. Where the spill is in a wadi channel, build the berm downstream before any other action.
2. **Recover.** Use vacuum truck or pumps to recover free liquid into a closed tank. Record the estimated volume recovered.
3. **Estimate the release.** The HSE Advisor estimates the release volume from the affected area, penetration depth and recovered volume. The estimate is recorded as a range if uncertain.
4. **Contaminated soil.** Excavate soil with visible hydrocarbon or produced water staining. Place it in labelled, lined containers or on a lined area for treatment or disposal. Excavation depth is confirmed by field screening.
5. **Wildlife and water.** The HSE Advisor checks for wildlife exposure and for any contact with water bodies or wadi flow. Any exposure is escalated immediately.
6. **Reporting timeline.**
   - Immediately: verbal report to the Production Supervisor.
   - Within 1 hour: HSE Advisor notifies site management and opens an incident record.
   - Within 24 hours: written incident note issued by the Production Engineer or HSE Advisor, including release volume estimate, cause hypotheses, deferred production and actions.
   - Regulatory notification: made according to the concession requirements and thresholds, by the HSE Advisor.
7. **Evidence.** Photograph the leak point, spill extent and failed component before any cleanup alters them. Keep failed pieces for failure analysis.

## Temporary clamp repair

A temporary clamp is a short-term measure only. It must not be treated as a permanent fix.

1. **Eligibility.** The Integrity Engineer confirms that a clamp is acceptable. A clamp must not be used if:
   - the defect is at a weld, tee, elbow or valve body;
   - the damage is circumferential or longer than the clamp can cover;
   - the wall around the defect is thinner than the minimum thickness set by the Integrity Engineer.
2. **Inspection.** Measure wall thickness on a grid around the leak point to define the sound area. Record all readings.
3. **Permits.** The HSE Advisor reviews the hot work or cold work permit, gas test certificate and isolation certificate. The line must remain depressurised, isolated and gas-tested before work starts.
4. **Preparation.** Clean the pipe surface to bare metal in the clamp area. Remove coating, scale and debris, and check the surface is free of pits that could prevent sealing.
5. **Installation.**
   1. Fit the clamp with seals centred on the defect.
   2. Tighten the bolts evenly in the sequence supplied by the clamp manufacturer, to the specified torque.
   3. Recheck torque after 30 minutes.
6. **Leak test.** Pressurise the line slowly to no more than 80 percent of the normal operating pressure and hold for at least 30 minutes. Inspect visually and with a gas detector.
7. **Limits of operation.** The Production Supervisor and Integrity Engineer set a maximum operating pressure, an inspection frequency (at least once per shift) and an expiry date for the clamp. The expiry date must not exceed 30 days without Integrity Engineer review.
8. **Work order.** The Maintenance Planner raises a work order for the permanent repair and links it to the incident record.

## Permanent repair and pressure test

1. **Repair method.** The Integrity Engineer specifies the repair: replacement of the damaged spool or section, or a qualified sleeve, in line with the applicable piping code and company integrity standards.
2. **Planning.** The Maintenance Planner schedules the contractor, materials and permits. Materials must have certificates that match the line specification (grade, wall thickness, pressure rating).
3. **Isolation and preparation.** Reinstate and verify isolation, purge the section, gas test and obtain the permit to work.
4. **Cut-out and fit-up.** Remove the damaged section. Retain it for failure analysis. Fit the new section with correct alignment and gap.
5. **Welding and inspection.** Welding is carried out by qualified welders under an approved procedure. All new welds receive visual inspection and non-destructive testing to the extent specified by the Integrity Engineer, normally 100 percent.
6. **Coating.** Restore the external coating or insulation to the original specification.
7. **Pressure test.**
   1. The Integrity Engineer issues the test procedure, giving the test medium, test pressure and hold time.
   2. Test pressure is normally 1.25 times the maximum allowable operating pressure unless the code requires otherwise.
   3. Raise pressure in steps, with checks for leaks at each step.
   4. Hold for at least 60 minutes with a stable pressure after temperature correction.
   5. Depressurise in a controlled manner and drain the test medium to the closed drain system or to an approved disposal route.
8. **Acceptance.** The Integrity Engineer signs the test record. Any pressure loss that cannot be explained by temperature is a failed test and the cause must be found before retest.

## Return to service

1. **Pre-start checks.** The Lead Operator confirms that:
   - all permits are closed and the work site is clear;
   - locks and tags are removed in the agreed sequence;
   - the pressure test record and weld inspection records are signed;
   - containment, spill cleanup and waste handling are complete or have an agreed completion plan.
2. **Authorisation.** The Production Supervisor authorises the restart in writing after review by the HSE Advisor and the Integrity Engineer.
3. **Line fill.** Open the manifold inlet valve slowly and pressurise the line in stages while the operator walks the line in daylight where practicable. Check flanges, clamps and the repaired section for leaks.
4. **Well restart sequence.** The Production Engineer defines the order in which wells are restarted. As a general rule:
   - ESP wells are restarted at the minimum stable frequency and ramped up gradually;
   - gas lift wells are restarted with injection raised stepwise;
   - Salt Flat Field wells are monitored for sand and gas surges on restart;
   - Wadi Field wells are checked for water cut after restart, with a sample taken at the first stable flow.
5. **Monitoring.** Monitor pressure, rate and gas readings at the repaired section for the first 24 hours. Operators check the area at least once per hour for the first 4 hours.
6. **Close-out.** The HSE Advisor closes the incident record when:
   - the permanent repair is complete and tested, or the clamp is approved with a documented expiry date and follow-up work order;
   - the final release volume and deferred production are recorded;
   - lessons learned are shared with all shifts.
7. **Records.** Keep the incident note, isolation list, permits, inspection data, pressure test record and restart log in the incident file for the period required by the company records policy.
