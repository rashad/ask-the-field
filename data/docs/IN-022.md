# Incident note: High separator level trip on W-10

**Document ID:** IN-022
**Event ID:** EV-0060
**Note date:** 2025-12-13
**Well:** W-10 (Field A, vertical, ESP)
**Severity:** Medium
**Status at note date:** Closed

## What happened
On 2025-12-13 the test separator high-level trip shut in W-10. The well was down for 5 hours and returned to production the same day. It is producing today.

## Immediate actions
- The Lead Operator confirmed the trip on the test separator and kept the well shut in while the cause was checked.
- The Field Engineer inspected the level controller and found its reading out of calibration.
- The controller was recalibrated and checked against the local gauge glass.
- The well was restarted once the level was stable and the ESP was running normally.
- The Production Supervisor was informed.

## Findings
The trip was caused by the level controller on the test separator, not by a well or ESP fault. The recalibration corrected the reading. No spill, leak or injury was reported.

## Impact
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Downtime | 5 hours |
| Uptime on the day | 19 hours |
| Oil on the day | 541.8 bbl |
| Gas on the day | 401 Mscf |
| Water cut on the day | 26.1 % |

For comparison, the 7 days to 2025-12-12 averaged 699.8 bbl/d oil, 506.7 Mscf/d gas, 24.4 % water cut and a GOR of 724 scf/bbl. The lower oil volume on the day matches the 5 hours of lost run time. Water cut was slightly higher, which is typical after a restart and should be watched over the next few days.

## Next actions
- The Production Engineer will review W-10 rates and water cut over the next few days to confirm they return to the prior baseline.
- The Maintenance Planner will add the test separator level controller to the next instrument calibration round.

## Procedure reference
No specific procedure applies to this event. The incident is closed.
